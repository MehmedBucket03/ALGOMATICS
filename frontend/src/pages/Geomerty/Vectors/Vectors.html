What is a Vector?

A vector is a mathematical object that has both magnitude (size) and direction. In geometry, vectors are often represented as arrows, where the length of the arrow indicates the magnitude, and the direction of the arrow shows the direction. For example, in 2D space, a vector can be described by its components along the x and y axes, such as (3, 4), which means it moves 3 units in the x-direction and 4 units in the y-direction. Vectors are used to represent quantities like displacement, velocity, and force.

What is a Dot Product?

The dot product is an operation between two vectors that results in a scalar (a single number). For two vectors A = (A_x, A_y) and B = (B_x, B_y) in 2D, the dot product is calculated as A · B = A_x * B_x + A_y * B_y. Geometrically, the dot product can also be expressed as A · B = |A| |B| cos(θ), where |A| and |B| are the magnitudes of the vectors, and θ is the angle between them. This operation measures how much one vector "goes in the direction" of another.

Why is the Dot Product Useful in Geometry?

The dot product is incredibly useful in geometry because it provides a way to measure the angle between two vectors (via cos(θ)), determine if vectors are perpendicular (if A · B = 0, the vectors are at 90°), and calculate projections (as shown in this visualizer). It’s also used in physics and computer graphics for tasks like calculating work (force · displacement), finding reflections, and determining lighting effects by measuring how much a surface faces a light source.
